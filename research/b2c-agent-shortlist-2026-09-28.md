# B2C AI products at $5–10/month

Research date: 28 September 2026. Revised brief: consumer-paid software, useful ongoing value, solo-founder implementation, no existing audience. All proposed prices, distribution channels, niches and build estimates are hypotheses. Advertised competitor prices establish a category and competition, not demand for a new entrant.

## Ranked experiments

1. **Daily workplace-English rehearsal for early-career non-native speakers — $8/month.** First audience hypothesis: Indian developers working with international teams. User records tomorrow's standup or explanation; app runs a short roleplay, identifies a few important improvements, saves recurring errors and schedules follow-up practice. Start with 20 carefully authored scenarios and turn-based audio, not unlimited live voice. Consumer buys for personal development, no employer purchase needed. Distribution: developer and English-learning creators, before/after demonstrations, free standup practice. Main risks: assessment quality, voice costs and strong competitors. [Yoodli](https://yoodli.ai/pricing) already offers individual Pro at $8/month billed annually with up to 10 roleplays weekly; simple roleplay is not differentiation. Value hypothesis: very specific situations, native-language explanations and a structured progression validated by language coaches.

2. **School-message-to-action assistant for parents — $6/month per household.** Parent shares a screenshot, PDF or newsletter. App extracts events, permission deadlines and bring/buy tasks, attaches source snippets, asks about ambiguity and supplies a confirmed daily/weekly agenda shared with another caregiver. Start with manual sharing and calendar export, not access to private message groups. Distribution: parenting creators and parent-to-parent sharing with permission. Main risks: missed dates, corrections, seasonal usage and family-data trust. [Ohai](https://www.ohai.ai/how-it-works/) already extracts household tasks and calendars from documents; its published individual price is $9.99/month. Hypothesis: narrow multilingual school-notice workflow for a particular parent community, not general family assistant.

3. **Weeknight meal execution assistant for a specific cuisine — $5/month.** First segment hypothesis: busy Indian vegetarian households or diaspora couples. Plan from tested recipes, available ingredients, time constraints and preferences; create consolidated grocery list, batch-prep steps and leftovers plan; adapt when plans change. Confirm inventory and quantities with the user. Distribution: recipe creators, actual weekly cooking demos, shareable household plans. [Eat This Much](https://www.eatthismuch.com/pricing) already offers weekly planning, groceries and leftovers at $5/month billed annually. [AnyList](https://www.anylist.com/complete) is much cheaper for lists and manual planning, $9.99/year individual or $14.99/year household. Premium hypothesis must be culturally specific execution and less manual upkeep, not generic recipe generation. Main risks: inventory input burden, recipe reliability and low willingness to pay. Avoid medical nutrition claims.

4. **Mistake-to-practice agent for one adult exam — $8/month.** Learner uploads a practice question, their answer and reasoning; app distinguishes concept gaps from careless errors, generates checked targeted practice, schedules review and tracks recurring mistakes. Begin with one subject/exam, original or licensed practice material and source-linked explanations. Distribution: exam educators and learner communities; free sample mistake diagnosis. [RemNote](https://www.remnote.com/pricing) advertises $8/month Pro annually and $18/month Pro with AI annually, and already provides quizzes, grading and study scheduling. Hypothesis: materially better exam-specific mistake diagnosis and low-friction practice, not PDF-to-flashcards. Risks: answer correctness, content rights and churn after exams; quarterly study passes may be more honest than expecting year-round subscriptions.

5. **Wear-your-existing-clothes outfit assistant — $7/month.** Start with 15–20 photographed items, user's comfort/style preferences, occasion and weather. Suggest outfits made from real inventory, track worn/unavailable items, assemble packing lists. Start with outfit collages, not generated virtual try-on. Choose a particular audience, such as modest workwear or first-job capsule wardrobes. Distribution: small fashion creators, outfit challenges and optional shareable boards. [Whering](https://whering.co.uk/how-it-works) already supports digital wardrobes and outfit planning. Hypothesis: excellent niche styling and simple onboarding can earn payment; not established. Risks: wardrobe setup burden, recommendation taste and free alternatives.

## Economics and validation

At $5/month, $10,000 MRR needs 2,000 paying accounts; at $8, 1,250; at $10, 1,000. These are gross subscription revenues before costs and refunds. Household plans count households, not each member.

Illustrative scenario, not benchmark: at 5% monthly paid churn, 1,250 subscribers require about 63 replacements/month just to stay level. At a hypothetical 5% activated-free-to-paid conversion, 1,250 payers require 25,000 activated users before allowing for churn. Pricing alone does not solve distribution.

Start with 20 target users, a working single-loop prototype and a real paid offer. Measure return use, completed outcomes, correction burden, cancellations, cost per active paid account and acquisition cost by channel. For $8 pricing, a proposed infrastructure/AI cost target is below $1–2 per typical paid user monthly, with explicit audio/import limits and abuse controls; this is a design target, not a verified provider-cost estimate. Avoid unlimited realtime voice or image generation before measuring heavy-user cost.

Suggested solo build estimates: 2–4 weeks for tightly scoped cooking or adult-study pilots, 3–5 weeks for short audio practice or school-document extraction. Assumes development experience and available test inputs; robust mobile apps, model evaluations, notifications, payments and integrations add work. No guaranteed revenue timeline.

Recommendation: validate workplace-English practice first if the founder relates to that audience; school-message assistant first if the desired product is household-oriented. Validate one repeat-use loop before building a broad personal assistant.

# Indian mid-cap and small-cap research shortlist

Research date: 27 September 2026. This is a business-quality watchlist, not a determination that these shares are attractively priced today.

## Coverage and limitations

The full AMFI January–June 2026 classification was downloaded to `amfi-june-2026.xlsx`; its rows are in `amfi-universe.csv`. It contains 150 mid-cap entries and 5,177 small-cap entries. AMFI includes exchange-listed instruments that are not necessarily actively traded ordinary shares, and its file is not a daily exchange listing reconciliation. Companies are represented once across exchanges.

Source: [AMFI classification file](https://portal.amfiindia.com/spages/AverageMarketCapitalization30Jun2026.pdf).

All ten classifications below were matched against that file. A comparable financial, liquidity, valuation and governance screen of every BSE/NSE security has NOT been completed. The other entries have not been rejected on fundamentals. No exhaustive ranking, current fair values, target prices or return forecasts are established here. Some evidence is FY26 even though newer quarterly filings exist. Full latest-quarter review, auditor/pledge/related-party checks and current valuation are outstanding before investment.

## Five mid-cap candidates

### Federal Bank — FEDERALBNK; AMFI rank 150

Investment hypothesis: deposit franchise, improving funding mix and healthier lending profitability could support earnings growth. Q1 FY27 net profit was ₹1,176.93 crore, up 36.57% year on year; net interest margin was 3.33%, net NPA 0.18%, and ROA 1.22%. Risks: credit losses, deposit competition and margin reversal. Valuation must be assessed against sustainable return on equity, not a low-looking P/E alone.

[Q1 FY27 company release](https://federal.bank.in/documents/10180/1148302360/Press+Release+Q1+FY+27.pdf/7e534416-1740-8651-83ae-bb60f04ca961?t=1784277402845).

### Ajanta Pharma — AJANTPHARM; AMFI rank 236

Investment hypothesis: branded medicines and differentiated product execution provide a route to growth beyond commodity generic pricing. FY26 revenue was ₹5,453 crore, up 17%; net profit ₹1,056 crore, up 15%. Risks: regulatory inspections, export/currency exposure, product competition and margin pressure. Verify latest quarter and regulatory observations before paying a premium for growth.

[FY26 company presentation/results](https://www.ajantapharma.com/assets/frontend/images/investors/files/statutory_communication/1777977352_BMOutcome05052026.pdf).

### Indian Hotels — INDHOTEL; AMFI rank 115

Investment hypothesis: hotel brands and expansion through management contracts can grow fee income without funding every new property. FY26 consolidated revenue was ₹9,971 crore, up 16%, and EBITDA ₹3,477 crore. Risks: travel cyclicality, weaker room pricing, expansion execution and valuation. Do not extrapolate peak hotel margins indefinitely.

[FY26 company release](https://www.ihcltata.com/press-room/ihcl-announces-financial-results-for-q4-and-full-year-fy-2025-26).

### AIA Engineering — AIAENG; AMFI rank 233

Investment hypothesis: specialised consumable grinding media and liners can benefit from mine conversions to higher-efficiency products. FY26 operating income was ₹4,419.86 crore and PAT after minority interest ₹1,270.16 crore; sales volume rose only about 1% to 258,002 tonnes. Non-operating income was material, so headline profit growth does not equal underlying volume growth. Risks: slow mine conversions, trade barriers, input prices and foreign exchange. Value the operating business separately from investment income.

[FY26 company presentation filed with NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/AIAENG_26052026154947_Letter.pdf); [company explanation of conversion opportunity](https://aiaengineering.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Transcript.pdf).

### Persistent Systems — PERSISTENT; AMFI rank 129

Investment hypothesis: software engineering, cloud modernisation and larger client deals can sustain expansion. Q1 FY27 dollar revenue grew 16.1% year on year to $452.4 million, with 16% EBIT margin. Higher rupee revenue growth partly reflects currency movements. Risks: client spending, AI-related pricing changes and a significant acquisition. Its Nagarro offer had secured 83.25% of outstanding capital by September 22; closing was expected by end-Q1 CY27, subject to approvals. Analyse acquisition financing and integration before investment; historical low debt would not settle that question.

[Q1 FY27 financial highlights](https://www.persistent.com/investors/quarterly-results/); [September 22 acquisition update](https://www.persistent.com/media/press-releases/persistents-takeover-offer-for-nagarro-successful/).

## Five small-cap candidates

### Computer Age Management Services — CAMS; AMFI rank 368

Investment hypothesis: mutual fund servicing infrastructure can participate in growth in financial savings. FY26 company-reported market share was approximately 68% of mutual fund assets serviced; EBITDA margin 45.11%, attributable profit ₹476.01 crore, with no external debt. Risks: fee pressure, market-linked assets, client concentration and technology/cybersecurity failures. Compare valuation with recurring earnings rather than assuming asset growth passes fully into fees.

[FY26 company annual report](https://www.camsonline.com/Annual_Report/FY2025-26/).

### City Union Bank — CUB; AMFI rank 348

Investment hypothesis: granular lending and improving asset quality could support profitable growth. Q1 FY27 net profit was ₹382.6 crore, up 25%; ROA 1.57%, gross NPA 1.73%, net NPA 0.61%. Risks: MSME credit stress, concentration and rapid lending growth. Gold loans were approximately 31% of advances. Price-to-book must be judged against sustainable profitability and provisioning needs.

[Q1 FY27 company presentation](https://cityunionbank.bank.in/filemanager/Jul26/CUB-INV-PPT-JUN-2026.pdf).

### eClerx Services — ECLERX; AMFI rank 402

Investment hypothesis: specialised outsourced operations, analytics and automation can win larger contracts. Q1 FY27 operating revenue was ₹1,152.4 crore, up 23.3%; PAT ₹164.3 crore, up 16%. Operating EBITDA margin was 23%, down 96 basis points year on year. Risks: client concentration, wage pressure, currency and AI replacing services. Require durable cash conversion and evidence that automation supports earnings rather than cannibalising them.

[Q1 FY27 company presentation](https://eclerx.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Quarterly-Investor-Presentation.pdf).

### CCL Products — CCL; AMFI rank 454

Investment hypothesis: instant-coffee manufacturing volume growth, available capacity and debt reduction can support earnings. Management reported FY26 volume growth of roughly 18–20%, profit ₹388.11 crore, up 25%, and March-end net debt around ₹1,073 crore. Risks: working capital, customer demand, commodity pass-through, currency and capital allocation. Coffee-price pass-through inflates revenue growth; track volume and profit per kilogram instead of headline sales alone.

[FY26 company earnings-call transcript](https://www.cclproducts.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Q4-Earnings-Call-transcript-2025-26.pdf).

### V-Guard Industries — VGUARD; AMFI rank 458

Investment hypothesis: electrical products and appliances offer a potential profit-recovery opportunity. FY26 revenue grew 7% to ₹5,965.78 crore; net cash was ₹231.16 crore at March end. But reported PAT fell 1.7% to ₹308.33 crore and ROCE declined to 16.1%. Risks: competition, weaker appliance margins and slow recovery. This is the most conditional inclusion: require sustained margin and profit improvement before treating it as a growth investment.

[FY26 company presentation filed with NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/VGUARD4321_12052026154330_Intimation.pdf).

## Decision boundary

Business performance does not establish a buy price. Before buying, reconcile September 25 exchange closing prices with split/bonus-adjusted diluted earnings, check all latest filings and disclosures, and model slower growth and lower valuation multiples. No current P/E/P/B or price targets are provided because those checks are incomplete. Suitability and allocation also remain dependent on the investor's horizon, emergency reserve and ability to bear losses.
